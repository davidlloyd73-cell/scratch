Road to 2028 Version 2

The 2026 House result was historic. Whether it is a short revolt against one president, damage to a party, or a lasting shift in the coalition is exactly what the data cannot settle yet. This model rebuilds the forecast on verified election returns and shows how much the answer depends on that one question.

Status: scenario tool, not a validated forecast. With seven historical cycles, the midterm swing does not predict the next presidential margin better than assuming nothing changes (see Validation). Watergate as a stress test, and what it cannot tell us

What the numbers show. The 1974 House margin was D+16.6 against D+5.2 in 1972 (Clerk of the House, official). Two years later 78% of that 11.4-point swing was still there (D+14.2 in 1976). By 1978 a third was left (D+9.0), and in 1980, the year of Reagan's presidential landslide, the House margin (D+2.5) was below its 1972 level. Democrats still won the 1980 House popular vote.

Reading it against the three mechanisms. Personal rejection of the president: the 1976 presidential result (D+2.2) was far narrower than the House result, which fits a party that held its congressional gains while the personal rejection faded. Party reputational damage: visible in 1974 and 1976, gone by 1980. Durable realignment: not supported by 1978–1980. These mechanisms are not separately identified by seven aggregate cycles, and the model does not claim they are. It lets you set their weights and see the consequence.

Where 2026 may differ. The 2026 swing is larger (16.0 against 11.4) and the electorate is far more polarised, so fewer voters are available to swing and fewer to swing back. Republican cohesion: Nixon lost his party's leaders and resigned, while Trump keeps his party's support. Media is fragmented, so a shared factual account of the controversies is harder. The 2028 Republican nominee will be a successor, as in 1976 (Ford), though Ford was an incumbent president and Vance would not be.

Limits. One analogue. The 1976 presidential baseline is distorted by the 1972 landslide, so the 1974 presidential ratio (2.2) is not used. Economic conditions in 1976–80 (inflation, the oil shock) are a competing explanation for 1980. Watergate informs this model, it does not determine its output: choose the pattern in the dial to see the effect.

Data integrity

Original data files are preserved read-only with checksums in the project repository (data/raw) and every cleaning decision is logged (data/CLEANING_LOG.md). The House file was supplied by the project owner from a distribution of MIT Election Data and Science Lab data (schema version 2025-09-10). Its provenance was not independently verified; its content was, against the Clerk's official totals (1992: +5.12 against +5.20 official) and against a known certified total (California 2018 district 1). Official Clerk totals for 1972, 1974 and 1992 were read from scanned volumes by eye. 2026 figures are incomplete and come from news reports of 3 to 5 November.
